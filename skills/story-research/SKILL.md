# Research the source

Establish what the story actually is before anyone draws anything. A film built
on a half-remembered plot cannot be fixed later.

## Search before you rely on memory

Search the web for the source text, its author, and existing scholarship or
summaries. For a traditional or non-English work, look for the original text
and for translations. Do not adapt from recollection — details you half
remember are exactly the details that carry the meaning.

If the user attached the text, read it directly and treat it as authoritative.

## What to establish

**Plot beats.** The story as an ordered list of beats, each one sentence. This
becomes the spine of the shot list. Note which beat is the turn and which is
the ending — those get the most screen time.

**Characters.** Every named or functional character: what they want, how they
behave, how they change. Note who is sympathetic and who is not, and resist
softening a villain the original does not soften.

**Setting.** Place, period, season, time of day. Real geography where the story
names it.

**Cultural context.** Who told this story and to whom. What it meant. What a
reader from that culture would recognise instantly and a stranger would miss.

**Visual detail from the text.** Objects, garments, buildings, tools, foods —
anything the source names. These become props and set dressing, and they are
what make the film feel like the story rather than a generic fable.

**Tone.** Comic, tragic, wry, moralising? Does it end happily? Many folk tales
do not, and flattening that ending is the most common way an adaptation
betrays its source.

## Flag what you do not know

List every word, object, custom or reference you cannot confidently visualise
or translate. Ask the user about them directly, in a single grouped question,
before moving on. This applies especially to:

- Words in the story's own language with no clean English equivalent
- Named garments, tools, dishes, buildings, instruments
- Social roles and institutions (who judges a dispute? who has authority?)
- Anything where a plausible guess would change the meaning

Do not proceed past this until the answers are in.

## Output

Write `canon.md` in the film folder containing the beats, characters, setting,
cultural notes and tone. This file is the story's authority for the rest of the
production; every later stage reads it rather than re-deriving the plot.

Present a summary to the user and confirm it is right before continuing.

Cite your sources.
